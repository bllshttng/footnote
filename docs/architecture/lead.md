# Lead: the tenured lead

A pass encodes a wave and steps_down (`/fno:lead <scope> --once`). A lead without `--once` stays. The operator asked for a lead that keeps working over a territory for days. It checks in on a schedule, drives with levers, and parks rather than dies. `/fno:lead <scope>` is that skill.

## What keeps a lead active

Three facts fix the design, all measured against the harness internals:

- The in-session lead arm reads BOARD truth. It blocks exit while actionable rows exist, and exits `NoWork` on a clean board or while it waits only on the user, CI or a worker. `NoProgress` remains the bounded fail-closed path.
- The beat is data. `agents.<harness>.beat` defaults to `auto`, which reads the harness capability row's `beat`. The row names `daemon`, `loop`, `goal` or `schedule`, and `fno-agents harness-beat` prints the result. On `loop` the lead injects the native `/loop` with `fno agents mail send '<command>' --to-self --raw`. The daemon's `lead_settle` arm mails every role on every harness, so quiet polling invokes no model anywhere.
- `lead.checkin_interval` is a 55-minute heartbeat under the one-hour prompt cache.

## The one arm, and the demand reads

The `lead_settle` arm is the one push arm. The daemon reads the org on its 300s beat. It mails the lead once per covered PR that settles green, and once per covered node that merges and closes. The lead arms no watch.

1. **Settle mail, 300s.** The daemon's `lead_settle` arm joins the org's owned nodes to their PR status reads. A green settled PR mails the lead the merge lever. A node that merged and closed mails the next-dispatch lever. A red settle stays with the daemon nudge ladder, which names the failing checks.

The deleted arms are demand reads. Each is read on demand. Mail arrives as a conversation turn and cannot be missed. The board and role liveness are check-in body reads. A red row in `fno agents status` stays the mechanical trigger for the one dispatch exception. Main CI is read as one verdict token (`red`, `green`, `pending`), never a check-run count. Several of the most productive lead wakes began with "main flipped green". Capacity is the spawn gate's job. The gate refused twice in the measured lead, correctly. The band's five readings changed no decision.

Every arm emits on probe failure as well as on the watched condition. When its instrument breaks, a silent monitor reports "nothing happened" and "the reader is dead" with the same silence.

Two things stay unmonitored because each has an owner. Worker transcripts belong to org mode watching. Per-PR CI belongs to the merge arm and the heal driver.

## The shape field and which hook reads it

A lead that spawns workers is not a pure pass. Until the shape field existed, saying so had no machine-visible act. The Stop nudge offered three options and detected two. The cheapest way to silence it, the carveout, downgraded a live teammate to advisory self-review. The fix:

- The lead manifest carries `shape` from birth. The default is `pass`. A lead declares `team` the moment it spawns its first worker, via `fno agents org shape <pass|team>`.
- `hooks/context-nudge.sh` resolves the manifest in its orphan branch. When the shape is `team` and the spawned workers are live, it goes silent. It stays loud for an unshaped lead walking away from live workers.
- `hooks/lead-postcompact-reinject.sh` appends the lead operating rules after a compaction. The manifest must name the compacting session.

## Term

A lead that never ends is not a bound. It is a lead nobody bothered to bound. One role ran 11 days, 37 context windows and 36 compactions with every bound looking correctly configured. The open-question branch returned `block` unconditionally. It never called the ceiling function the other branches share. The fix: the manifest carries `term`, a spec of `span:<N>[smhd]` or `compactions:<N>`. Undeclared reads a Rust constant default, `span:96h` (the eval's measured ideal handoff, about 100 hours in). `fno agents org term <spec> [--reason TEXT]` declares or extends it. A declared or reached term refuses a bare re-declaration without `--reason` - the extension is the receipt.

The Stop-hook gate (`loopcheck::lead_decide`) reads the term before the board read. It sits ahead of every early return, including the open-question branch that used to wire completion shut. Reached or unreadable, it blocks through the same bounded spine every other block uses (`blind_block`). A lead that ignores the message still ends on `Budget` and escalates. The message names the handoff: `fno agents spawn --promote <scope> --hand-off`, or an extension with a reason. A `compactions:` term only measures a claude transcript. Declaring one on another harness is refused at declaration time, not discovered later as an always-`Unreadable` gate.

A same-session re-role (`fno agents org init --scope --force`) starts a fresh term: the re-role is itself a grantor's receipt. A re-scope or widen by the same session is different. It carries the term, the lead start and the compaction count through the lead clock on the role name record. The verdict keeps counting the lead's real hours. `fno agents org verdict` prints the term as evidence (`term: span:96h (default) 101h of 96h, reached`) and the lead start beside it. A reached term does not change the verdict word itself. Only the Stop-hook gate forces the handoff.

## Compaction

The role survives a compact. Its evidence does not. After a compaction the session context holds a summary, and nothing forces that summary back against the store before the lead acts on it. Two machines close the gap. The post-compact hook (`lead-postcompact-reinject.sh`) reads the newest `isCompactSummary` entry from the transcript. It orders by line position, never by timestamp. It extracts the node ids the published grammar matches and resolves every candidate through one `fno backlog get` batch. The answer rides back as `unresolved:` and `resolved:` rows with one instruction: act on these rows, not on the summary. The stop gate adds a check beside the term gate. Past `lead.compaction_ceiling` (default 3) compactions since the manifest's `created_at`, a role handoff doc that is missing or more than 24 hours old blocks exit. The block names the refresh command, `precompact-canon-doc.sh`. The doc gate counts boundaries only on claude transcripts. It fails open on anything it cannot measure, because it blocks exit and a false block traps a session. One reader-authority rule covers both machines: a node's state comes from `fno backlog get`, never from a `graph.json` read. With the sqlite backend the file is the relational store's stale twin.

After a compaction, Claude re-injects each skill body as the session first read it. So a step that a later ruling removed from the skill comes back. The check-in's `skill_drift` reader compares the newest `invoked_skills` row with each skill's file and raises `attention:` on a mismatch. When a ruling ships as a skill edit, it expires at that merge, so a rulings readout cannot carry this. Codex and other non-claude leads read `READER FAILED skill_drift` until the transcript resolver covers them.

## Stop semantics

Exit is blocked while actionable rows exist. The stop hook reads board truth. A clean board, or a board waiting only on the user, CI or a worker, exits `NoWork`. The next heartbeat or mail wakes it, and the daemon's settle mail is mail. `NoProgress` after three unshrinking fires escalates automatically and the session parks. The operator's answer wakes it through the wake arm. A lead never fights the hook.

## The dispatch exception and its journal row

A lead does not dispatch. The single exception is a provably dead dispatching arm: a red row in `fno agents status`. That spawn is journaled `lead_dispatch_exception`, naming the arm and the node, BEFORE it fires. A spawn without that row is a defect. The journal row is emitted with:

`fno doctor event emit -t lead_dispatch_exception -s loop -d '{"scope":"<scope>","arm":"<arm>","node":"<id>"}'`

## The check-in journal: write canonical, read it back

Every `lead_checkin` row carries the canonical keys `scope` and `change`, plus the beat's measured evidence under distinct extra keys. The validator refuses any other shape. The aliases `role_scope`, `role`, and `result` are refused even beside the canonical keys. Before this contract, 153 check-ins from five leads named the scope three different ways. No schema-keyed reader can walk a journal like that.

`fno agents org checkin` runs the check-in body as one verb. It gathers the readings the skill names and prints them in a fixed order. It diffs the previous canonical row for the scope. It emits the `lead_checkin` row from the same dict it printed, so the row and the lines cannot disagree. Coverage is per reading. A reader that fails prints `READER FAILED <name>: <reason>` on its own line. The beat continues without it. The row names the failure under `readers_failed`. `coverage` counts the readers that answered. The printed total is `coverage` plus the failed count, so it always equals the readers the beat ran. `--change` carries the lead's sentence, and the derived diff moves to `diff`. When a scope's newest check-in is older than two check-in intervals, the next stop journals one mechanical row with source `hook`. The row carries the previous fire's measured facts. A cancelled role writes none. `no change` is refused while any reader failed, because an unread axis cannot be known unchanged. If journalling was requested but no row was written, the verb exits 3 after printing the beat. The verb never decides. It holds no graph write, no spawn and no reap. The levers stay the lead's judgment. The skill's check-in body is this verb's documented output contract.

`fno agents org history` reads one role's rows back, newest first, complete payloads. It never generates a summary. Legacy alias rows count as rejected evidence. It never silently accepts them. It reads the `events.db` store beside every journal ``paths.event_journals`` resolves, and it ingests before it reads. The store holds every durable row that the files' single rotation generation cannot keep on disk. The read is an index seek on `(scope, type, ts_ms)`, not a scan of raw rows. A zero-match answer still names every store it read, with each journal's ingested and lead-row counts. An empty history is a measurement, not an absence. `fno agents org -n` answers a different question: who holds which role right now.

## The three role numbers

Three different role counts travel under similar words, and collapsing them is how a board reads clean while two rows hold one scope. A manifest split is the role manifest naming a different session than its registry row (`manifest_session` != `registry_session`). The check-in renders it as `manifest-splits` and the ledger page shows the `splits` tile. A double rule is more than one non-terminal registry row on one normalized territory key. The check-in renders `double-ruled` plus a line naming the scope and both live holders. The ledger page shows the `double ruled` tile and flips the verdict card to its disagrees state. When a registry row flips terminal, nothing clears its role fields. A stale role is that terminal row still carrying role fields. Stale roles accumulate until `fno agents rm <row>` or the next titled spawn over that scope clears them. The check-in renders `stale-promoted` with the remedy command, and the ledger page shows the `stale roles` tile without flipping the verdict. The stale row can be a claude session whose transcript ends in a tool call with no result. Then the line names the session id, the tool and the call's time. When the call predates the last boot, the line adds the boot time and offers `fno agents resume <row>` beside `fno agents rm <row>`. After a reboot the claude harness decides which of its background jobs to relaunch. fno relaunches none, so a titled session left down shows here. fno never writes the result the harness writes on an interrupt, because the transcript belongs to the harness. The org and the role-split reader read the STORED registry status. `fno agents list` renders served activity computed from a live probe instead. The two surfaces can disagree about the same row without either lying.

## The verdict: bounds read as a set

`fno agents org verdict` reads the lead's tenure bounds as one set and answers `converging`, `stalled`, `degraded`, or `unknown`. This reader exists because a role ran seven days with every bound looking correctly configured while the lead sat outside all of them. 15 Budget ceiling hits told nobody, because a Budget terminal ended a turn and never escalated. `respawn_count` never moved on a lead that does not dispatch. The block-cap variable was injected only into spawned workers. No reader compared any of the numbers.

Four bounds, each with a state of `exceeded`, `within`, or `absent`. `iterations` reads loop fires against the manifest's `budget_max_iterations`. When `respawn_ceiling` is 0, `respawns` reads absent. Otherwise it reads `respawn_count` against `respawn_ceiling`. For Claude, `compactions` counts the transcript's own `compact_boundary` lines at or after the role start. It falls back to post-compact `context_snapshot` rows in the journal. Other harnesses keep the journal reading. `block_cap` reads the `loop_check_config` recording, absent with no row. An absent bound never prints as satisfied. An unset bound and a satisfied bound are different readings. A org role older than two check-in intervals with no recent loop check-in reads `unknown`, not `converging`.

Role history lives in the one SQL event store beside each journal. There is no per-rundown (rejected 2026-09-17: a second store for rows the SQL store already keeps forever). Every store open stamps a coverage epoch, `events_meta.coverage_complete_since_ms`: the first moment a build with coverage stamped the store. History proven complete starts there. `fno agents org history` prints its proven horizon from the per-store coverage receipts and marks earlier history unknown. Its JSON carries per-journal `coverage` plus a top-level `complete_since`. When any store is unknown or unreadable, that value is null. The verdict compares the role's `created_at` with that proven start and reports `lifetime: measured` or `lifetime: partial`. Under `partial`, every count is a lower bound. A count bound under its ceiling reads `unmeasured`, never `within`. A lower bound over a ceiling still reads `exceeded`. The block cap is a recording, not a count over history, so coverage does not touch it. A scoped `lead_checkin` survives a rotated-away live journal because the store answers, not the file. The delivery trend splits the role scope at the manifest `created_at`. Rows created before it are INHERITED. Rows created after are FILED by this lead. A lead that files real work raises the raw undelivered count by working well. So `stalled` keys only on the inherited set. The last fire read a quiet board. Inherited nodes sit undelivered. None closed inside the window of three check-in intervals. Filing never makes a lead read stalled.

The verdict also reports five hygiene checks from the holder's transcript: claim-before-read, spawn/departure exit, role verification, PR-watch kickoff, and context timing. When the holder reads `fno agents org` before its first ruling, an inherited role passes role verification. A promotion call also qualifies. Delivery attribution starts at `generation_start`, the earlier of holder registry-row birth and manifest `created_at`. The check-in and verdict readers cap each transcript at 4 MiB. The lead eval fold reads the whole transcript. A missing, unreadable, or over-cap transcript is `unmeasurable`, never clean. Hygiene is evidence and never changes the verdict or bound summary.

One owner assembles the verdict's inputs. The native `lead-verdict` verb resolves the caller role, the canonical scope, the role manifest, the config values, the graph scope, and the window. It reads the inherited/filed delivery split. It scans the journals, decides the verdict, and renders the JSON payload and the human page. The config keys are `lead.checkin_interval` and `lead.compaction_ceiling`. Every refusal names the failed reading, and none degrade to zero. An explicit scope reads that scope's own manifest at `<space>/leads/<scope>.md`, not the caller's. Python keeps only the Typer transport, the event-path enumeration, the binary resolution, and the durable operator-channel writes. The escalation question's words render natively beside the same inputs.

`degraded`, `stalled`, and `unknown` escalate through `fno agents org escalate <scope>`. It records one deduplicated operator question naming the verdict, the exceeded and absent bounds, and the handoff offer. The offer names the succession primitive: `fno agents spawn --promote <scope> --hand-off`. The operator pulls the trigger. A Budget terminal now escalates exactly like NoProgress. A failed verdict read records `verdict unreadable` rather than going silent. The lead never spawns its own successor.

## Succession

`fno agents spawn --promote <scope> --hand-off` runs the handoff as one transaction, and the journal answers whether it completed. The team-settle that answers the spawn emits `team_succession_announced`, one fleet announcement from the successor's name, and `team_succession_transferred` beside the registry commit it already makes. The successor's first check-in beat emits `team_succession_verified`, then `team_succession_released`, or `team_succession_release_unproven` naming the row still seated. It then emits `team_succession_retro_filed`: the intel writer launches detached for the predecessor's session and the row names the eval dir it writes. A succession whose successor died unbound reverts in the reap sweep as before, and the revert now announces itself from the predecessor's name beside `team_succession_reverted`. Every step row carries one `succession_id`, the pending record's ts plus the scope, so one grep reads the whole handoff. A failed announce never undoes the transfer: the registry commit is the authority. The handoff point is per harness: `lead.handoff.<harness>.compaction_ceiling` wins over `lead.compaction_ceiling`, over a built-in default of claude 3, codex 12, and 3 for any other harness. The dead `lead.compaction_ceiling` key is gone.

## Lead eval

`fno-agents intel --session <sid> --windows` folds a lead transcript in one streaming pass: a claude session transcript, or a codex rollout found in `$CODEX_HOME/sessions` by the session id. It counts tools, errors and their classes, refusal buckets, typed and machine wakes, tokens, merges, spawns, check-ins, and hygiene. Compaction boundaries open windows, and `--since`/`--until` bound which timestamped rows are included. A claude window opens at a `compact_boundary` row. A codex window opens at a `compacted` row, and its summary is that row's `payload.message`. Codex tokens are per-window deltas of the cumulative `token_count` total, so a window reads what the lead spent inside it, not the session sum. A codex error is a tool output starting `Script failed` or `Script terminated`, or an inner result with a non-zero exit code. Codex subagent rollouts are separate rollout files the fold does not read, so a rollout reports zero subagent files. The compaction ceiling splits windows 1 through ceiling+1 inside the bound from later windows after it.

`--write [dir]` creates an index, `part1-metrics.md` through `part4-reforms.md`, `data/fold.json`, and each window's compaction-summary file. It creates only missing files. Existing eval files are kept unchanged and reported as `kept <file>`. The lead fills in failures and reforms from the metrics and timeline, filing each reform as a backlog node. When a lead has ended, the `term_eval` daemon arm runs independently. After the last check-in is 30 minutes old, it skips live role holders and sessions that already have an eval. Check-in rows key on `holder_session`, the holder's harness session id. The older `data.session_id` holds a loop job id and names no session. A session with no transcript under either harness is skipped as already written. It processes at most one due session per 600-second tick in a child process.

## The rundown page

`fno agents org rundown` renders `pages/rundown.html` (`--out` overrides) as the Rundown page: the per-team rundown of everything going on. The masthead carries the generated stamp. The verdict card has four states: the org agrees with itself, holds no live roles, cannot be read, or disagrees with itself. It shows count tiles and the readers line. A Chief heads the page as a banner. Its teams are the sections below. One section per Lead or Head renders in title order. It shows the stored title, holder, scope, status dot, agree and source tags, and the needs-the-user list from the board's operator_question rows. An unreadable queue shows its reason instead. Stats, status bar, legend, and an Active territory table follow. Two more sections name untitled epics and Unassigned leaves (parentless, actionable, contained by no node). The one-release `/role` route serves the same file, and the old `fno agents org rundown` spelling still writes `term.html` by default. Only active nodes render as table rows. Settled members show as counts in the bar and legend. The data path is the org's own. `gather_team` and the native org fold read run in Python. The page assembly runs in the native `term-ledger` verb (the lead-history split, so the Python-tree ratchet holds). The page renders that answer and never re-derives the scope join. The ledger, `fno agents org -n`, and the fold cannot disagree about who holds a node. When the served file is older than five minutes, the `/role` bridge route starts one background `fno agents org rundown` render. The render is single-flight, so concurrent reads start no second child. The route always serves the current file at once. The daemon's `role_ledger` arm runs `fno agents org rundown` every 300 seconds. Each run writes one `control_plane_tick` row. The file on disk is at most one beat old while the daemon runs. The page carries the shared reload script, `crates/fno-agents/src/page_reload.js`. A tab that stays visible and untouched for `backlog.page_reload_s` seconds (default 60, 0 is off) reloads itself. It keeps its scroll position, form values, pressed chips and open sections. The page prints its generated stamp and a relative age, never its own stale verdict. An empty org renders "no live roles" as a measurement. An unreadable registry renders the named reason. An unresolved fold states why in place. Never a blank or falsely healthy page.

## Manifest-only roles and the dead-role reaper

The Rundown page reads the worker roster once per render. Each active node lists its current workers and folds former graph sessions below them. Worker folds show context usage and measurement age, activity and model basis, runtime, unread mail, and any request awaiting the operator. Missing readings say `unmeasured`. A failed roster read names its reason on each team card.

When no live row holds its scope, the org lists its manifest. The org drops a manifest only under the reaper's full vacate predicate: the holder is proven dead and the role is older than the window. So a young role whose holder died stays listed, and the empty-role alarm can page on it. Only the newest manifest naming a session counts, so a re-scope leftover is not a role. Liveness keys on the session id, and new manifests carry no `owner_pid`. A org-listed manifest-only role whose holder is not proven dead is dropped and named by scope in the lead wake's tick note. The wake never roots or wakes it. A dead role belongs to the reaper.

Three facts name a manifest-only role a DEAD ROLE. No live registry row carries its territory key. The manifest's holder session is proven dead. The proof is one of three witnesses. The claude roster lists the session in a terminal state. Its roster pid is gone. Or the roster is a clean full read that does not list the session while its transcript has been quiet past the window. The manifest itself is older than the window. The window is three check-in intervals, twelve hours at the default. Every other case is unknown and keeps the role. The keep names its reason: an unreadable or partial roster, a missing transcript, a non-claude harness, a holder on the roster in any non-terminal state. A role older than the window keeps unless the holder is proven dead. A holder dead for an hour keeps until the role itself passes the window.

The daemon retire arm owns the reaper. Each tick runs the dead-role sweep before the registry sweep. The manual `fno agents reap` verb runs it beside its own report, under the `roles` key in its JSON and as `would vacate` lines under a dry run. The vacate re-checks both facts under their locks. A manifest rewritten to a new session mid-sweep refuses the write. A live row that took the scope refuses it too. Stale role fields on terminal rows over the vacated scope clear in the same registry write. The receipt names who inherits: the live role one rung up whose scope covers the dead one, else the operator. The sweep grants no role. A vacated epic falls back to its project's territory by construction.

`fno agents org done --scope <scope>` stays the attended override: it needs no death proof and vacates whatever holds the scope. One case the reaper does not cover: a role held by a live-status row whose session is dead. That case belongs to the lead wake's successor path under its respawn ceiling.

## Role names

A role carries a name its lead picks at the first beat: `fno agents org checkin --name <name>`, 2 to 24 letters, unique among live roles. The name belongs to the role (law d-1726ee1c, reversible), keyed on the canonical scope in `~/.fno/agents/role_names.json`, beside the registry. Not in the lead manifest, which 3 of 5 live roles lacked on 2026-09-23 and which Python rewrites with `force=True` at every role moment. The file shape is frozen and documented at the top of `crates/fno-agents/src/role_names.rs`. The mux reads the same file contract the way it reads the registry, and a missing or malformed file reads as no names there. Records key the holder on the harness session id (law d-e952ed19), `null` until a succession successor's first beat binds it. A `--hand-off` succession adds a generation number (Barnaby, Barnaby II) at the role-settle apply. A fresh grant forgets the record. A told-to re-scope keeps the name, generation, theme and title with `--keep-name-from <old-scope>`. It is refused unless the old record's holder session matches the new holder. The record also carries the lead clock, `term`: the keying session, the scope, the arm stamp, the lead start, and the declared term. A re-scope by the same session reads the lead's real hours. A successor or a same-scope re-arm starts fresh. Check-in rows carry `holder_session`. The previous beat is read by holder first, then by scope. A duplicate live name is refused naming the holder and scope. The dead-role sweep prunes records whose scope has no live role or whose holder session no longer matches. That sweep is also the closer for a stale record left by the attended Python role paths (`fno agents org promote`, `reclaim_role`), which do not call role-settle. Readers: the check-in's role line and bind step, and the org fold's per-scope `name` field (`fno agents org --json -n`). Also the rundown card's holder line. The sideline carries no bracket tag. When the record has a title, a titled row's line 2 shows it. Otherwise the line shows the scope. Until the org verb ports to Rust, plain `fno agents org` carries no name.

## Titles and theme

People read a level title, not a level number: L0 is "Chief of <portfolio>", L1 is "Head of <project>", L2 is "Lead of <theme>" (the org-title rename). An L2 lead names the running theme of its epics once per role at check-in, beside its name: `fno agents org checkin --theme <theme>`. A re-scope carries the theme and its title, so growing the epic list never drops the rank back to the raw scope text. The title rides the stored record. It shows in mail ranks (`from_rank="Lead of native backlog"`), the sideline card's second line, the feed's role column and owner text, and the rundown page's section headings. Mail sent before the upgrade still injects. The door accepts the legacy `L{level} {scope}` rank for the one-release window. A rank the live record does not support is refused.

## The codex limit

Codex has no native heartbeat or watch launcher. Its externally owned wake arm is the pulse. The beat table names this limit and the other harness-specific choices.

## Config keys

`config.lead` carries the injected check-in text, so an OSS user edits one place. `lead.checkin_interval` defaults to `55m`. The verdict window is three intervals and the hook missed-beat row is two intervals, so the defaults read 165m and 110m. A legacy `lead.goal_text` key is ignored by the config block. A fresh install runs with no config.
